VoxVector

An AI-assisted vocal and audio deception detection platform being developed to analyze interview and conversational audio for evidence relevant to potential deception.

VoxVector is being built for deception detection. The current runtime is a functional observational research foundation; scientifically validated deception inference is not yet enabled.

Open VoxVectorOpen canonical dossierExplore Labs

Product objective

Deception detection from vocal and conversational evidence.

Future validated inference is designed to combine multiple evidence families, uncertainty, reliability, convergence, conflict, and alternative explanations.

Current status

Status: Active development

Maturity: Functional observational research foundation

Scientific state: No validated deception inference currently active

Architecture

Eligibility and reliability → evidence collection and analysis → evidence convergence → candidate classification → final disposition gate.

The current candidate state is indeterminate and fails closed to abstention or insufficient evidence.

Current analytical scope

The primary pipeline currently integrates RMS and intensity, zero crossing rate, spectral centroid and spread, fundamental frequency and harmonicity, F0 and intensity dynamics, harmonic to noise ratio, spectral flux and rolloff, formant candidate tracking, pause topology, optional response latency, optional transcript disfluency observations, and optional within speaker baseline deviations.

Evidence principles

  • Reliability is an eligibility control, not a deception score.
  • Observations are preserved with provenance and quality context.
  • Evidence convergence and conflict remain visible.
  • Alternative explanations remain represented.

Future detection stack

  • Richer acoustic and voice quality descriptors
  • Linguistic and conversational analysis
  • Learned audio representations
  • Validated multimethod deception classifiers
  • Calibrated uncertainty and abstention

Validation gate

Future validated inference requires task and population definition, speaker disjoint evaluation, out of sample testing, leakage and robustness analysis, calibration and uncertainty assessment, reliability and abstention testing, and external replication where feasible.
