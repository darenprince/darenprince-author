## Investor interpretation

The current approximately **$3.50M central strategic assessment** is intentionally higher than estimated recreation cost because an investor or acquirer would not be buying disconnected source files. The current asset includes a coherent case-centered architecture, a 21-stage analytical workflow, provenance and reliability controls, reusable analysis components, provider abstractions, operational infrastructure, QA and audit discipline, a deep research/method framework, product UX, documentation, and a differentiated visual identity.

The valuation is intentionally lower than the milestone ranges because the largest remaining discounts are also clear: no verified recurring revenue, no independently established large rights-cleared proprietary corpus, no independently established proprietary validated classifier, incomplete end-to-end connected MVP proof, unresolved enterprise-security work, and scientific validation that remains a future program rather than a current capability.

The investor thesis is therefore **proof-driven rather than feature-count-driven**. The next meaningful valuation gain comes from reducing execution, commercial, data, security, and scientific uncertainty in that order.

## Value credited today

The current assessment credits evidence-backed value for:

- the canonical VoxVector software and case-centered architecture;
- implemented and built analytical foundations without promoting research candidates to production capability;
- provenance, auditability, reliability, evidence-state, and abstention-oriented design;
- the React product workspace and Developer Console as implemented product/operational surfaces where supported by current source;
- live/persisted infrastructure and observability evidence where independently inspected;
- the D01–D32 method and research taxonomy, validation architecture, and documented R&D program as intellectual architecture;
- technical documentation, QA discipline, and reproducibility controls;
- brand, visual identity, product design, and commercialization preparation where ownership/right-to-use is supportable;
- monetization and licensing optionality as strategy, not as current revenue.

## Value not credited without new evidence

The current assessment assigns **no additional valuation premium** merely for the existence or possibility of:

- proprietary trained deception-classifier weights that have not been independently established;
- a substantial rights-cleared labeled deception corpus that has not been independently established;
- patents, pending patents, registered trademarks, or other formal registrations not documented in the diligence record;
- customer contracts, annual recurring revenue, net revenue retention, paid subscriptions, or active production customers not documented in the diligence record;
- App Store or Google Play distribution not independently verified;
- research candidates, planned methods, configured providers, or successful software execution being treated as scientifically validated deception inference.

Unknown items are recorded as **unverified**, not automatically assumed absent. They should be added to the valuation only after evidence is preserved in the appropriate diligence record.

## Proprietary IP and transaction-value protection

Investor value depends not only on creating intellectual property but on making ownership, permitted use, and transferability clear enough to survive diligence.

### Crown Labs background IP

Commercial agreements should preserve Crown Labs ownership of lawfully owned pre-existing and independently developed VoxVector materials, including original application code, orchestration logic, original schemas and result contracts, original documentation, original product/brand assets, internal evaluation tooling, and proprietary research/evaluation assets where those rights are actually held.

A license, pilot, research engagement, API agreement, or enterprise deployment should grant only the rights expressly stated in that agreement. Access to VoxVector should not by itself transfer ownership of the underlying platform, method taxonomy, source code, model/evaluation assets, or future improvements.

### Non-public proprietary assets

Non-public source, model weights, evaluation fixtures, benchmark outputs, research annotations, internal roadmaps, security information, customer-specific integrations, and rights-cleared datasets should be handled as confidential proprietary material when Crown Labs has a lawful basis to do so.

The repository contains public material. Publicly disclosed source or documentation must **not** be described as a trade secret merely because Crown Labs created it. Trade-secret treatment applies only to information that is actually maintained as non-public and protected with appropriate confidentiality measures.

### Third-party and open-source boundaries

Crown Labs ownership language must always carve out third-party libraries, open-source components, external model weights, external datasets, hosted providers, and other materials governed by separate licenses or terms. No investor, customer, or licensee should be told that Crown Labs owns third-party technology merely because VoxVector integrates it.

A transaction-ready diligence package should maintain an up-to-date dependency, model, dataset, provider, and media-rights register with the applicable license or contractual basis for each material third-party asset.

### Customer data and derived assets

Customer or research-participant audio, transcripts, labels, annotations, and case records should not be treated as Crown Labs training property by default. Contracts and consent/data-rights documentation should define ownership, processing rights, retention, deletion, de-identification, permitted model/evaluation use, and publication rights.

Rights to aggregated or derived technical metrics should be defined explicitly and only used where the contract and applicable law permit it. A proprietary data moat should be valued only when the company can demonstrate lawful collection, provenance, labeling, retention, and reuse rights.

### Chain of title and brand rights

Before a financing, license, or acquisition process, Crown Labs should maintain evidence for contributor assignments, contractor work-for-hire or assignment terms where applicable, trademark/brand ownership or usage rights, domain ownership, and commercial-use rights for major visual assets. This page does not assert that every such item is already complete.

### Restricted-use and reverse-engineering controls

Enterprise, API, OEM, white-label, research, and managed-service agreements may include confidentiality, redistribution, competitive-use, extraction, reverse-engineering, benchmarking-publication, and model/data-exfiltration restrictions where lawful and commercially appropriate. Those controls are contractual protections; they do not convert public material into confidential IP or override third-party license rights.

Legal counsel should review operative agreement language before external execution.

## Monetization posture

The near-term commercial wedge is **auditable vocal/audio evidence intelligence and professional workflow**, not an automated universal lie verdict.

Potential revenue layers include subscriptions, professional/team workspaces, enterprise licensing, API usage, managed analytical services, research/evaluation engagements, institutional/private deployments, and later task-specific validated inference offerings if scientific evidence supports them.

The following ranges are **internal planning assumptions from the valuation audit, not current list prices, booked revenue, or customer contracts**:

| Commercial model | Illustrative planning range |
|---|---:|
| Professional workspace | $250–$500/month/user-equivalent |
| Advanced/team workspace | $1.5K–$3K/month/team |
| Enterprise/institutional | $50K–$150K+/year ACV |
| API | Usage-based plus potential minimum commitment |
| Managed analysis | $1.5K–$7.5K+/case |
| Research/validation engagement | $25K–$150K+/project |
| OEM/private deployment | $50K–$250K+ base plus usage/support |
| Future validated task-specific tier | Premium enterprise pricing only after validation |

Final pricing should be set only after compute cost, support burden, implementation effort, security requirements, willingness-to-pay, retention, and production capability are measured.

## Valuation catalysts

Revalue VoxVector when material evidence changes, especially when one or more of the following occurs:

1. A controlled, provider-backed golden case completes end to end on an exact revision with persisted and reopenable artifacts.
2. Report/history/reopen and authenticated desktop/mobile product proof are complete.
3. Known security/privacy diligence findings are remediated and enterprise controls improve.
4. External paid design partners sign, use the product, renew, or produce measurable workflow value.
5. A rights-cleared, provenance-rich, speaker-disjoint proprietary dataset is established.
6. Task-specific model evaluation demonstrates speaker-disjoint and out-of-sample performance with calibrated uncertainty and abstention.
7. Independent replication or external expert review supports defined claims.
8. Recurring revenue, retention, gross-margin, pipeline, or enterprise-contract evidence becomes available.
9. Material IP registrations, assignments, exclusive rights, or defensible proprietary model/data assets are documented.

A catalyst should change the valuation only to the extent that it changes evidence, transferability, commercial economics, or risk.
