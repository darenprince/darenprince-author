VoxVector is being built to detect and evaluate potential deception in interview and conversational audio through structured, reliability-aware, multimethod evidence analysis.

The platform does not reduce a person's voice to a single stress signal or a dramatic score. It is designed to determine whether the recording is usable, measure what can actually be observed, identify where evidence converges or conflicts, and ultimately support validated task-specific deception classification.

The intelligence layer

Not one signal. A body of evidence.

VoxVector's analytical foundation separates measurable evidence families so the system can reason about convergence, conflict, reliability, and alternative explanations instead of treating one vocal behavior as proof.

Acoustic & spectral

Measures the physical characteristics of the recorded signal and its spectral behavior.

  • RMS and relative intensity
  • Zero-crossing rate
  • Spectral centroid and spread
  • Spectral flux and rolloff

Voice & prosody

Tracks vocal dynamics and voice-quality observations across time.

  • Fundamental frequency
  • Harmonicity and HNR
  • F0 and intensity dynamics
  • Formant candidate tracking

Timing & language

Adds conversational structure when the required timing or transcript context is available.

  • Pause topology
  • Response latency
  • Transcript disfluencies
  • Within-speaker baselines
How VoxVector thinks

Four stages. No shortcuts.

The architecture deliberately separates technical eligibility from evidence collection, candidate classification, and final disposition.

01 / ELIGIBILITY

Can this audio be trusted?

Assess recording quality, usable signal, timing context, speaker separation, transcript quality, and other constraints before interpretation.

02 / EVIDENCE

What can be measured?

Extract supported observations and preserve their provenance, quality state, and comparison context.

03 / CANDIDATE

Do observations converge?

Organize evidence into provisional hypotheses while preserving conflict, dependence, uncertainty, and alternatives.

04 / DISPOSITION

Has the evidence earned inference?

Apply validation and reliability gates. Otherwise abstain or report insufficient evidence.

Current analytical scope

Built from measurable methods.

The active primary pipeline already provides a substantial observational foundation. These measurements are evidence inputs, not individual proof of deception.

Acoustic dynamicsRMS, intensity, zero crossing, F0, harmonicity, HNR
Spectral analysisCentroid, spread, flux, rolloff and cepstral processing
ProsodyF0 range, dispersion, slope, endpoint change and intensity dynamics
Temporal behaviorPauses, pause topology, speech rate, articulation rate and latency
Voice qualityFormant candidates, jitter/shimmer utilities and harmonic observations
Conversational contextTranscript disfluency, interaction timing and within-speaker baseline comparison
Scientific boundary: stress, hesitation, pitch, silence, arousal, emotion, cognitive load, speaking rate, pauses, or any other individual feature does not prove deception. VoxVector's intended detection capability depends on validated multimethod evidence, reliability, uncertainty, and task-specific calibration.
Research → validation → product

The detection engine is a destination, not a claim.

VoxVector preserves research candidates while making their maturity explicit. The product objective remains deception detection throughout the development path.

Expand the evidence

Richer acoustic and glottal-source descriptors, learned audio representations, temporal models, stronger channel controls, ASR, alignment, linguistic structure, repairs, consistency analysis, and speaker-aware baselines.

Validate the inference

Define the target task and population, use speaker-disjoint evaluation, test out of sample, examine leakage and recording conditions, assess calibration and uncertainty, and test abstention and robustness.

Controlled deployment

Only validated task-specific methods may progress to inferential deception classification, calibrated probability, confidence matrices, evidence disposition, and governed production workflows.
