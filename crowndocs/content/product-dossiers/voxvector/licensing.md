# VoxVector Licensing

## Licensing Philosophy

VoxVector can operate as both a standalone analytical application and a modular vocal/audio intelligence infrastructure layer.

Commercial structures should maximize licensing flexibility while preserving ownership clarity, scientific integrity, auditability, and transaction value.

Commercial rights must distinguish Crown Labs-owned materials from third-party libraries, open-source components, model weights, datasets, hosted providers, and other services that remain governed by their applicable licenses and terms.

The current investor-facing valuation posture is synchronized in [VoxVector Current Valuation Assessment](../../../../VoxVector/docs/CURRENT_VALUATION_ASSESSMENT.md).

## Professional and Enterprise Licensing

Potential licensing may include individual or professional subscriptions, organization-wide deployments, API access, audit and provenance controls, managed infrastructure, integration support, private environments, service-level commitments, and restricted-use enterprise agreements.

Contracts must not represent research candidates, provider configuration, successful software execution, or unvalidated deception inference as scientifically validated performance.

Enterprise agreements should define, as applicable:

- licensed users, seats, environments, or usage limits;
- permitted and prohibited use cases;
- customer data ownership and processing rights;
- retention and deletion obligations;
- confidentiality and security obligations;
- support and service levels;
- deployment responsibilities;
- audit/provenance requirements;
- publication/benchmarking rights;
- model/data use rights;
- ownership of integrations, configuration, and improvements;
- termination and post-termination data handling;
- regulatory or high-stakes-use restrictions.

## Research Licensing

Research agreements may support controlled access to analytical modules, reproducibility tooling, evaluation workflows, benchmark fixtures, and approved integrations.

Research terms should define dataset provenance, permitted use, publication rights, confidentiality, human-subject or consent obligations where applicable, evaluation protocol, derivative-work rights, and intellectual property ownership.

Research access does not convert a candidate method into a validated product capability.

## API, OEM, and Embedded Licensing

A mature VoxVector API may be licensed for embedding into third-party analytical workflows while preserving the canonical result contract, reliability states, provenance, abstention behavior, scientific-status metadata, and version traceability.

API/OEM agreements should avoid transferring the underlying platform unless an explicit asset sale or assignment is intended. Typical structures may grant bounded rights to use outputs or services while retaining Crown Labs ownership of the underlying platform and lawfully owned background IP.

White-label or embedded deployment should not erase required attribution or notices for third-party components where their licenses require them.

## Intellectual Property Protection

### Crown Labs background IP

Commercial structures should preserve Crown Labs ownership of lawfully owned pre-existing and independently developed VoxVector materials, including original application code, architecture, orchestration logic, schemas/result contracts, original documentation, original product/brand materials, internal evaluation tooling, and proprietary research/evaluation assets where those rights are actually held.

A subscription, pilot, API license, research engagement, OEM agreement, or enterprise deployment should grant only the rights expressly stated in the agreement. Access to VoxVector should not by itself transfer ownership of the underlying platform, source code, method taxonomy, internal evaluation assets, model/evaluation materials, or future independently developed improvements.

### Non-public proprietary assets and confidentiality

Non-public source, model weights, evaluation fixtures, benchmark outputs, research annotations, internal roadmaps, security information, customer-specific integrations, and rights-cleared datasets should be treated as confidential proprietary material when Crown Labs has a lawful basis to do so and actually protects their confidentiality.

The repository includes public material. Publicly disclosed code or documentation must **not** be described as a trade secret merely because Crown Labs created it. Trade-secret claims should be limited to information that remains non-public and is subject to appropriate confidentiality controls.

### Third-party and open-source carve-outs

Ownership language must expressly carve out third-party libraries, open-source components, external model weights, external datasets, hosted providers, and other materials governed by separate licenses or service terms.

Crown Labs should maintain a transaction-ready register of material dependencies, models, datasets, providers, and media/visual assets, including the applicable license, source, contractual basis, and redistribution/commercial-use restrictions.

No customer, investor, or licensee should be told that Crown Labs owns a third-party technology merely because VoxVector integrates or orchestrates it.

### Customer data, training rights, and derived assets

Customer or research-participant audio, transcripts, labels, annotations, and case records should not become Crown Labs training property by default.

Contracts and consent/data-rights documents should define:

- ownership of source recordings and case content;
- processing purpose and permitted use;
- retention and deletion;
- de-identification or aggregation;
- whether data may be used for product improvement, benchmarking, evaluation, or model training;
- publication/research rights;
- export/return obligations;
- restrictions for sensitive or regulated data.

Rights to aggregated or derived technical metrics should be defined explicitly and exercised only where contract and law permit. A proprietary-data valuation premium should be claimed only when collection, provenance, labeling, retention, and reuse rights are demonstrable.

### Improvements, feedback, and custom work

Agreements should distinguish:

- Crown Labs background IP;
- customer background IP;
- configuration or implementation work;
- customer-specific deliverables;
- general platform improvements;
- feedback and feature requests;
- jointly developed work where applicable.

Ownership should not be left to implication. Contract language should state what each party owns and what license, if any, the other party receives.

### Brand, marks, and presentation rights

Use of the VoxVector or Crown Labs name, logo, visual identity, screenshots, reports, and product marks should be licensed separately from underlying software rights where appropriate.

This documentation does not assert that every mark is registered. Trademark-registration status, domain ownership, visual-source rights, and brand chain of title should be verified in diligence before being represented as formal registered IP.

### Restricted use, redistribution, and reverse engineering

Enterprise, API, OEM, white-label, research, and managed-service agreements may include restrictions on redistribution, resale, competitive replication, extraction, model/data exfiltration, reverse engineering, circumvention, public benchmarking, publication of sensitive technical details, and sublicensing where lawful and commercially appropriate.

These are contractual controls. They do not override statutory rights, third-party licenses, or convert public source into confidential information.

### Chain of title

Before financing, licensing, or acquisition diligence, Crown Labs should maintain evidence for contributor assignments, contractor work-for-hire or assignment terms where applicable, major visual/brand rights, domain ownership, and rights to proprietary datasets/evaluation assets.

This document does not assume those records are complete merely because the underlying asset exists.

## Scientific and Claims Protection

Licensing language should protect enterprise value by preventing the commercial contract from making stronger scientific claims than the canonical evidence supports.

Contracts, proposals, exhibits, and marketing materials should distinguish:

- observational measurements;
- evidence synthesis;
- candidate classification;
- validated task-specific inference;
- final disposition.

Where a validated inferential model is not established for the relevant task/population, the agreement should not describe VoxVector as a scientifically proven lie detector or guarantee deception-detection accuracy.

This claim discipline protects the brand, reduces legal/regulatory exposure, and preserves the credibility required for future validation.

## Transaction Readiness

A financing, strategic-license, or acquisition data room should eventually contain, as applicable:

- source/code ownership schedule;
- contributor and contractor assignment records;
- third-party dependency/license register;
- model and dataset rights register;
- customer/data-rights terms;
- trademark/domain/brand-rights evidence;
- privacy and retention policies;
- security assessments;
- material provider agreements;
- current scientific-status statement;
- current valuation assessment and evidence date.

Legal counsel should review operative agreements before execution. This document defines product and investor policy, not final legal language.
