# Licensing Structures

## Purpose

This framework defines how Crown Labs software systems, intellectual property assets, frameworks, language engines, research assets, and operational infrastructure may be licensed, distributed, or integrated commercially.

The objective is to preserve intellectual-property control, ownership clarity, scientific integrity, and transaction value while enabling scalable monetization and strategic partnerships.

## Licensing Philosophy

Crown Labs products are designed as modular systems.

This allows:

- standalone deployment
- partial licensing
- embedded integrations
- enterprise adaptation
- white-label deployment
- API distribution
- managed-service delivery
- research/evaluation access

Licensing structures are intentionally flexible so systems can scale without silently transferring foundational ownership.

A license should grant only the rights expressly stated in the agreement. Access to a Crown Labs product does not by itself transfer source ownership, background IP, proprietary methods, non-public evaluation assets, brand rights, future independently developed improvements, or customer-independent platform technology.

## Primary Licensing Categories

### Consumer Software Licensing

Applies to:

- executable applications
- desktop utilities
- subscription software
- creator tools
- standalone consumer products

### Enterprise Licensing

Enterprise licensing supports:

- organizational deployment
- internal infrastructure usage
- private hosting
- restricted enterprise integrations
- managed deployment agreements
- support and service levels
- governed data handling

Enterprise agreements may include:

- annual licensing
- seat-based licensing
- infrastructure support
- dedicated deployment environments
- custom implementation agreements
- API or workflow integrations
- audit/provenance requirements
- retention and deletion controls

### API Licensing

Several Crown Labs systems are structurally compatible with API distribution.

Potential API licensing categories include:

- language reconstruction
- behavioral analysis
- vocal/audio intelligence
- reporting systems
- monitoring infrastructure
- AI-assisted processing

API agreements should define usage limits, permitted use, support, rate limits, data handling, output rights, confidentiality, security obligations, and any restrictions required by high-stakes or scientifically bounded use cases.

### White-Label and OEM Licensing

Certain systems may be licensed under white-label or OEM agreements.

This allows external companies to deploy Crown Labs infrastructure within their own products or branding while preserving the ownership of the underlying Crown Labs platform except where an explicit assignment is intended.

White-label rights should remain separate from source ownership, model/data rights, and brand rights. Third-party attribution or notice requirements must remain intact where applicable.

### Restricted-Use Licensing

Some systems may require restricted-use agreements due to:

- forensic functionality
- behavioral or vocal analysis capability
- evidence-continuity infrastructure
- sensitive operational use cases
- regulated or high-stakes environments

Restricted-use licensing may limit, where lawful and appropriate:

- redistribution
- modification
- resale
- sublicensing
- competitive replication
- reverse engineering or circumvention
- model/data extraction
- publication of sensitive benchmark results
- public disclosure of protected technical information
- use outside the validated or contractually permitted scope

Contractual restrictions do not override statutory rights or third-party license terms.

### Research and Evaluation Licensing

Research agreements may provide controlled access to analytical modules, benchmark fixtures, reproducibility tooling, evaluation workflows, and approved data/model interfaces.

Research terms should define:

- permitted purpose
- data provenance and rights
- confidentiality
- publication rights
- benchmark-disclosure rules
- intellectual-property ownership
- derivative-work rights
- consent or human-subject obligations where applicable
- scientific-status and claims boundaries

Research access does not convert an experimental method into a validated production capability.

### Publishing and Educational Licensing

Books, frameworks, and educational assets may support:

- educational licensing
- training distribution
- institutional usage
- workshop licensing
- curriculum integration

## Intellectual Property Protection

### Background IP

Commercial structures should preserve Crown Labs ownership of lawfully owned pre-existing and independently developed background IP unless an agreement expressly assigns specified rights.

Background IP may include, where ownership is established:

- original source code
- architecture and orchestration logic
- original schemas and result contracts
- original documentation
- internal evaluation tooling
- proprietary research/evaluation assets
- original brand and product materials
- reusable infrastructure and frameworks

A product license should not silently transfer the reusable Crown Labs platform merely because custom implementation or integration work is delivered to a customer.

### Public Material and Trade-Secret Boundaries

Crown Labs repositories and documentation include public material.

Publicly disclosed source, documentation, diagrams, or methods must **not** be described as trade secrets merely because Crown Labs created them. Trade-secret or confidential-treatment value applies only to information that is actually non-public and subject to appropriate confidentiality and access controls.

This distinction protects credibility in financing, licensing, and acquisition diligence.

### Non-Public Proprietary Assets

Non-public source, internal model weights, evaluation fixtures, benchmark outputs, research annotations, security information, internal roadmaps, customer-specific integrations, and rights-cleared datasets may be treated as proprietary/confidential when Crown Labs has a lawful basis to do so and actually protects their confidentiality.

Confidentiality obligations should match the sensitivity and commercial importance of the asset rather than relying on broad ownership language alone.

### Third-Party and Open-Source Carve-Outs

Crown Labs ownership language must carve out third-party libraries, open-source software, external model weights, external datasets, hosted providers, media assets, and other materials governed by separate licenses or service terms.

No customer, investor, or licensee should be told that Crown Labs owns third-party technology merely because a Crown Labs product integrates, calls, orchestrates, or deploys it.

A transaction-ready licensing record should identify material dependencies, models, datasets, providers, and major visual/media assets together with the applicable license, source, contractual basis, and redistribution/commercial-use restrictions.

### Customer Data and Training Rights

Customer, research-participant, or partner data should not become Crown Labs training property by default.

Contracts should expressly define, as applicable:

- ownership of source data and case content
- processing purpose and permitted use
- retention and deletion
- de-identification or aggregation
- product-improvement rights
- benchmarking/evaluation rights
- model-training rights
- publication rights
- export/return obligations
- restrictions applicable to sensitive or regulated data

Rights to aggregated or derived technical metrics should be stated explicitly and exercised only where the contract and applicable law permit them.

A proprietary-data valuation premium should be claimed only when provenance, collection rights, labeling rights, retention rights, and reuse rights are demonstrable.

### Improvements, Feedback, and Custom Work

Agreements should distinguish among:

- Crown Labs background IP
- customer background IP
- customer-specific configuration
- custom deliverables
- general platform improvements
- feedback and feature requests
- jointly developed work where applicable

Ownership and license rights should not be left to implication. If the parties intend an assignment, exclusive license, non-exclusive license, or retained reusable improvement right, the agreement should state it.

### Brand and Trademark Rights

Use of Crown Labs or product names, logos, screenshots, reports, and brand assets should be governed separately from underlying software rights where appropriate.

This framework does not assert that every product mark is registered. Registration status, domain ownership, visual-source rights, and brand chain of title should be verified before being represented as formal registered IP.

### Chain of Title

Before financing, licensing, or acquisition diligence, Crown Labs should maintain evidence, where applicable, for:

- contributor assignments
- contractor work-for-hire or assignment terms
- source/code ownership
- major brand/visual rights
- domain ownership
- proprietary dataset/evaluation rights
- third-party dependency and model/data licenses

An asset should not receive an exclusivity or transferability premium merely because it exists. The relevant rights must be supportable.

## Scientific and High-Stakes Claims Protection

Licensing language must not make stronger scientific or operational claims than the underlying product evidence supports.

Contracts and proposals should preserve the distinction among:

- observations and measurements
- evidence synthesis
- candidate classification
- validated task-specific inference
- final disposition

A configured provider is not a proven provider execution. Successful software execution is not scientific validation. A deployment is not browser verification. A research candidate is not a production capability.

Where a validated inferential model is not established for the relevant task and population, an agreement should not promise universal deception detection, guaranteed accuracy, or a scientifically proven lie verdict.

## Strategic Licensing Position

Licensing is treated as a long-term strategic multiplier.

The ecosystem is intentionally designed so:

- infrastructure can be reused
- intelligence layers can be embedded
- systems can reinforce one another
- products can scale independently
- ownership remains auditable
- data/model rights remain explicit

Shared value should not be double-counted across every product in an investor valuation.

## Transaction Readiness

A mature licensing data room should contain, as applicable:

- source/code ownership schedules
- contributor and contractor assignments
- third-party dependency/license registers
- model and dataset rights registers
- customer/data-rights terms
- trademark/domain/brand-rights evidence
- privacy and retention policies
- security assessments
- material provider agreements
- product scientific-status statements
- current valuation assessments and evidence dates

Legal counsel should review operative agreements before execution. This framework defines strategic licensing policy, not final legal language.
