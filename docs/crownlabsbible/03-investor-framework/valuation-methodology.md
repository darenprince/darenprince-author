# Valuation Methodology

## Purpose

This framework defines how Crown Labs products, systems, intellectual property assets, and platform ecosystems are evaluated internally for strategic positioning, investor communication, licensing discussions, and acquisition modeling.

The objective is to maintain consistent valuation logic across all company assets.

## Core Valuation Philosophy

Crown Labs products are not valued solely on present revenue.

Valuation analysis also considers:

- strategic positioning
- technical uniqueness
- ecosystem leverage
- scalability
- licensing potential
- intellectual property ownership
- infrastructure maturity
- audience alignment
- deployment readiness
- category differentiation

## Primary Valuation Categories

### Current Operational Value

Represents the estimated present value of the product based on:

- existing infrastructure
- development maturity
- verified deployable functionality
- operational readiness
- documented architecture
- current market positioning

### Projected Completed Value

Represents estimated future valuation assuming:

- production completion
- successful deployment and runtime verification
- monetization activation
- ecosystem integration
- user acquisition
- strategic growth

Projected value is not current value and must remain tied to explicit assumptions and milestones.

### Liquidity Value

Represents the estimated fast sale or rapid acquisition value of an asset under compressed timelines.

This category prioritizes:

- transferable infrastructure
- usable codebase
- branding maturity
- commercial usability
- immediate deployability

### Strategic As-Is Value

Represents the value of acquiring the assembled system today rather than recreating it component by component.

Strategic as-is value may include a premium over recreation cost for:

- integrated architecture and accumulated technical decisions;
- reproducibility and auditability;
- documented research and product systems;
- time-to-market avoided;
- brand and product maturity;
- licensing optionality;
- reusable infrastructure;
- ecosystem fit.

It must also reflect discounts for incomplete product proof, transfer friction, founder dependence, scientific uncertainty, missing proprietary-data rights, security/compliance gaps, and absent commercial traction where applicable.

## Strategic Multipliers

Certain products increase the value of other systems across the ecosystem.

These products are treated as strategic multipliers.

Examples include:

- CrownCode.ai
- LumiLogix
- AI Cherry Pie
- CrownCode Intelligence Suite

These systems provide shared intelligence, workflow acceleration, language processing, or orchestration capabilities that amplify downstream products.

Strategic multiplier value must not be double-counted across both the shared platform and every consuming product.

## Product Maturity Influence

Valuation generally increases when products move from:

- concept
- to prototype
- to tested/verified deployment
- to monetized infrastructure
- to validated task-specific capability where scientific validation is relevant

Products with active deployments, verified functionality, reproducible evidence, and proven infrastructure receive higher strategic weighting. Scientific validation is a separate dimension and should be credited only when the applicable task-specific evidence exists.

## Intellectual Property Weighting

Intellectual property value is influenced by:

- originality
- scalability
- framework ownership
- brand strength
- licensing adaptability
- cross platform applicability
- long term ecosystem integration
- chain of title
- exclusivity
- transferability
- third-party-license constraints
- lawful proprietary data/model rights where established

Publicly disclosed source or documentation should not be assigned a trade-secret premium. Non-public assets may receive confidentiality/trade-secret weighting only where the information is actually kept non-public and protected with appropriate measures.

Unverified patents, registered marks, proprietary model weights, datasets, contributor assignments, or exclusive rights receive no incremental premium until the diligence record establishes them.

## Product-Level Current Valuation Records

A product may maintain a dated internal current-valuation record when:

1. the valuation date and repository/evidence revision are stated;
2. current value is separated from milestone and scenario value;
3. the record identifies what is and is not credited;
4. operational and scientific states remain governed by their own canonical documents;
5. unverified revenue, customers, data, model, patent, or licensing claims are not assumed;
6. material updates are synchronized with affected investor/product mirrors.

## Investor Communication Standards

All valuation discussions must:

- distinguish current value from projected value;
- distinguish replacement, liquidity, strategic, milestone, and scenario value;
- avoid unsupported claims;
- separate conceptual systems from operational systems;
- separate software verification from scientific validation;
- separate deployment from browser/runtime verification;
- remain commercially realistic;
- maintain legal defensibility;
- identify material assumptions and evidence dates.

## Revenue Influence

Revenue is important but not treated as the sole valuation metric.

Additional weighting includes:

- market category potential
- enterprise licensing opportunities
- SaaS scalability
- infrastructure reuse
- ecosystem integration
- acquisition positioning

Once meaningful recurring revenue exists, revenue quality becomes increasingly important, including retention, expansion, gross margin, implementation burden, customer concentration, sales efficiency, and contract durability.

Modeled ARR must never be presented as current ARR.

## Long Term Valuation Strategy

The long term objective is not simply to build isolated applications.

The strategy is to create a layered ecosystem where:

- products reinforce one another
- shared intelligence increases efficiency
- brand trust compounds over time
- infrastructure becomes reusable
- intellectual property becomes increasingly defensible

The valuation framework should reward evidence that reduces technical, scientific, commercial, legal, security, and transferability risk rather than rewarding unsupported feature breadth.
